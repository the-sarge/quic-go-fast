# E4 handshake, ACK and PTO emission receipt

The concrete `packetEmission` owner interprets ACK and PTO opportunities and owns their construction, registration and queue handoff, plus normal coalesced handshake/0-RTT emission. The connection consumes progress, blocked reasons, pacing deadlines and capacity wakeups. It keeps the initial run-loop capacity guard before handshake-MTU fallback and applies fallback again before each PTO continuation. The ordinary/GSO loop and its receive fairness are preserved.

The owner borrows the existing packer, recovery and queue slots. Synchronous typed hooks keep first-send/idle facts, Initial-key retirement, qlog and path-generation metadata with the connection. Long-header activity precedes registration; client Initial-key retirement follows Handshake registration before another packet can be built. A coalesced UDP datagram still notifies the connection-ID manager once. There is no new worker, timer, completion message, shadow accounting or per-packet allocation.

## Ownership and finite evidence

The domain is the existing supported coalesced encryption levels, ACK/PTO send modes, empty-content outcomes and typed handshake feedback. Existing packer, wire and TLS code owns representation. Ordering and lifetime obligations are universal inside the migrated construction/handoff paths; wire and performance observations are example-level. Runtime migration is shipped behavior; constructor cleanup is required safety enforcement. Tests and disposable capture scripts are verification aids, while this receipt and the frontier transition are metadata. No aid is a maintained product deliverable.

Contract closure applies to the plan’s fourth ownership row. Private allocating constructors reclaim unreturned byte storage on every error, including empty ACK output; successful output transfers storage to E2’s queue owner. Append-style ownership, frame callbacks and already consumed packet numbers are preserved. Cleanup remains inside each allocating constructor; no protocol state is refunded. No guard mutation was required because deterministic regressions first observed reference count 1 and now observe 0 before pool reuse.

Queue startup, shutdown, replacement and direct path writes remain connection-owned until their assigned slices. The old ACK/PTO and pacing/queue mock-interaction checks are replaced by real-packer outcomes. The ordinary pacing test retains its timer-wakeup assertions; broader lifecycle and handshake tests remain as preservation coverage.

The trace covers coalesced/ACK/PTO constructors, synchronous connection effects, recovery registration, queue metadata, capacity and continuation scheduling. Direct-probe disposal, retained-close bytes, receive ownership and physical-link/application performance are explicitly untraced. No public API, wire format, keyspace, MTU or congestion policy changed.

## Performance comparison

The initial candidate is `f0401b52ebc231f8e3f2769e84d2daa6f838b92d`; the parent is `1bb6df8ef4eff369a3e9821010f420afa4920287`. Both builds use identical native Go 1.27.1 and fixture source. The connection remains 1216 bytes, with no added setup allocation. Focused allocations are unchanged: 37 per ordinary batch and 104 per three-packet GSO batch. No cold-close change belongs to E4.

The existing shared-process handshake benchmark completed ten alternating paired one-second samples on four dedicated physical cores with GOMAXPROCS=4 and benchmem. Its geometric mean time ratio is 1.005156, with a one-sided 95% upper bound of 1.005904, passing the 1.05 limit. Allocations remain 2081–2082 per handshake on both variants. The paired resampling uses 20000 whole-pair samples with seed 20260907.

The initial P3 campaign completed all ten alternating pairs with valid payload, probe and host-qualification results. [Initial analysis](initial-summary.json) establishes goodput lower bound 0.999395, CPU/unit upper bound 1.003054, bytes/unit upper bound 1.000115 and zero bad-probe difference. Probe p99 has geometric mean ratio 1.066396 and upper bound 1.086273, failing the 1.05 requirement. The host’s temporary restrictions were restored. This campaign is retained and is not relabeled or pooled with its replacement.

Compiled-path inspection found extra 48-byte result copies and progress merging in the new ordinary-opportunity continuation loop. That establishes avoidable work, not a proven latency cause. The one permitted in-contract revision restores a direct ordinary return and the original bounded recursive PTO continuation, preserving the emission owner, registration and cleanup. Runtime revision `f149127631874187b7b5255041a75d576bd1392c` passes the focused and race regressions and lint. The complete replacement handshake/P3 campaign passes. The failed initial campaign is retained separately; no samples were pooled, excluded or replaced within either campaign. Both campaigns and the single revision exhaust the accepted measurement budget.

The [replacement analysis](summary.json) reports ten valid P3 pairs: goodput lower bound 0.998098, CPU/unit upper bound 1.002839, bytes/unit upper bound 1.000639, probe p99 upper bound 0.968297 and bad-probe upper difference 0. No payload errors or failed probes occurred. One baseline run missed one scheduled probe; it remains in the reliability ledger and paired analysis. The [replacement handshake benchmark](benchmark-summary.json) has geometric mean ratio 1.008823 and upper bound 1.009273, passing the 1.05 limit. Focused allocations remain 37/104 per ordinary/GSO batch, with the same 1216-byte connection value. Linux full uncached tests, focused races and vet also passed on this revision.

Both campaigns use four physical cores and GOMAXPROCS=4 per endpoint: client cores 8–11, server 12–15, with SMT siblings 24–31 reserved. The controller is pinned to core 0, monitor to core 1, and other system/user/machine work to 0–7 and 16–23. P3 uses native Linux loopback, established QUIC v1, active GSO, qlog off, 1071-byte DATAGRAMs at 4 Gbps, 2 s warmup, 60 s measurement, 1 s drain and ten alternating pairs. The qlog-on paired smoke is diagnostic only. Host contention stayed within the frozen guest/sibling thresholds; each phase’s restoration receipt confirms that temporary restrictions and reservation state were removed. This qualifies the finite Linux comparison; it does not prove the dispatch change was the sole cause of the initial difference or claim physical-link performance.
