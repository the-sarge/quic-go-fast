# Head-index receive queue under paced arrivals

## Question and scope

Does the experimental head-index receive slice preserve useful delivery and queue latency at plausible bulk-transfer rates while reducing allocation churn? This is a new queue-level investigation following the [unpaced Linux experiment](https://github.com/the-sarge/quic-go-fast/pull/13), whose concurrent workload dropped about 98% of offers. It does not reopen completed D2, authorize a runtime merge, or certify an application/network deployment.

The baseline is main `686bce6541438101c96732a023984e031faf6df7`. The candidate reuses only the head-index runtime change from `5731f248f991c267e3490d7bf6f3e01cf2d6db1b`. Both retain the mutex, lazy storage, admission limit of 128, drop-new behavior, payload copies, FIFO ordering, wakeup, cancellation, and close semantics. The common measurement aid is Linux-only and requires the `queue_experiment` build tag plus explicit environment opt-in. It is experimental evidence, not a maintained product test or benchmark gate; retire it with this investigation unless a later accepted contract adopts it.

## Workload assumptions fixed before collection

The repository documents 1,071-byte records and bulk transfer, but supplies no measured datagram arrival trace. These rates and pauses are provisional synthetic scenarios, not observed production distributions. Test one queue with one producer and one receiver at 10,000, 100,000, and 500,000 datagrams/sec: respectively 85.68, 856.8, and 4,284 Mbps of application payload, excluding transport and link overhead. Each rate has three shapes: individual arrivals, bursts of 32, and bursts of 32 with a requested 1 ms receiver sleep every 10 ms. The last shape models a scheduling or downstream pause; it does not reproduce disk or decode work. Actual sleep duration is measured because the OS can oversleep. The receiver otherwise performs only payload/FIFO checks and timestamp accounting.

For orientation, an empty 128-entry queue represents 12.8 ms, 1.28 ms, or 0.256 ms of arrivals at those rates. This predicts that a 1 ms pause can overflow the high-rate queue even when queue operations themselves are fast. Burst granularity, occupancy before the pause, and actual pause length affect the observed loss.

Use two logical CPUs on separate physical cores of minimax (12 and 13), `GOMAXPROCS=2`, the existing governor and Go 1.26.0, native precompiled test binaries, and the same payload/measurement code in both variants. The producer busy-waits against an absolute monotonic schedule to avoid millisecond timer batching. It skips elapsed slots instead of emitting a catch-up burst; every skipped offer is reported separately from a queue drop. The process CPU metric includes the busy pacer and is not queue CPU cost. This affinity is not exclusive host ownership.

Each sample warms the queue by filling and draining 128 entries, allocates histogram storage, and forces GC before recording memory/CPU counters. A fixed two-second arrival window is followed by close, draining admitted messages, and joining the receiver. Each offered payload carries its sequence and enqueue-attempt timestamp. Record FIFO/integrity failures, offers, generator skips, delivered messages, queue drops, achieved rate, queue latency (enqueue attempt through Receive return, including lock wait), burst-deadline lateness, actual receiver pauses, bytes/allocations per delivery, GC cycles/pause, and process CPU including pacing. Timestamp and histogram work is shared instrumentation, not production receiver work. Quantiles are upper bucket boundaries at 1 µs resolution; overflow at 100 ms is explicitly counted, and means/maxima are not clipped.

## Finite collection and interpretation

Before timed collection, build both native binaries, run focused datagram tests on both, and run short instrumented race smokes for both. Smokes validate the harness; they are not analysis samples. Fix correctness or harness defects before freezing the source. Preserve exact source and binary hashes and verify that only `datagram_queue.go` differs between the frozen variants.

Collect exactly 20 paired rounds for each of the nine cases, two seconds per sample (360 samples). Alternate baseline/candidate order by round and rotate case order. Record every command, environment, UTC start, exit status, stdout/stderr, boundary frequency, and selected-core/sibling activity. The collector refuses an existing manifest. Do not exclude samples, extend collection, or rerun cases based on their results. A failed process stops collection and remains in the manifest.

Report each case's median achieved rate, generator-miss percentage, queue-drop percentage, p50/p99 queue latency, allocated bytes and allocation count per delivered message, GC activity, and CPU with its pacing caveat. Report paired candidate/base geometric means and seeded 10,000-resample percentile bootstrap 95% intervals for positive metrics, and paired absolute differences for loss and generator-miss percentage points. Use seed 20260906. These exploratory intervals describe variation in this fixed host/window, not universal equivalence or a production decision gate. In particular, do not reuse the prior borderline 5% cutoff as a reason to repeat testing.

A sample qualifies as having generated its requested rate when measured offered/sec is at least 98% of target; retain and visibly flag every sample that does not qualify. Any case with an unqualified sample has only achieved-rate evidence and cannot establish behavior at its nominal rate. Generator misses, burst lateness, and actual pauses remain visible even when within this tolerance. Fewer delivered messages can reduce total allocation and GC; compare memory per delivered message, not per offered input. No runtime promotion follows automatically from this experiment. Capture the bounded answer, limitations, raw evidence, and candidate patch for review.
