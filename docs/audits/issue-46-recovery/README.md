# Controlled handshake-corruption recovery: issue #46

**Result: bounded corruption recovers; continuous Handshake corruption reaches the caller's deadline while probes continue. No transport defect was demonstrated.** The follow-up adds five permanent behavior cases in [handshake_corruption_test.go](../../../integrationtests/self/handshake_corruption_test.go): Initial recovery, its undamaged control, Handshake recovery, its undamaged control, and Dial deadline expiry under continuous Handshake corruption. The original randomized MITM test, production code and its timeout/corruption/transfer assertions remain unchanged. The historical failures in [#46](https://github.com/the-sarge/quic-go-fast/issues/46) remain unresolved.

## Authorization and provenance

After the [first investigation](../issue-46/README.md), the maintainer approved building deterministic fixtures, tracing reliable-delivery counterfactuals, and retaining permanent tests or making a narrow fix only if a transport obligation is shown to fail. The [plan](PLAN.md) records the accepted seams, hypotheses and predeclared executions. This supersedes the earlier proposal to observe ten routine CI invocations; that observation window was never installed or started.

The feature branch `codex/issue-46-recovery-fixtures` starts at `16423f270ebcc3e0cb784b87033d6d73bcf49819`; its runtime source is still `901f53e0fcc8e8e5ff004de4f84a78728a61a150`. Work used `/Volumes/worktrees/quic-go-fast/issue-46-recovery-fixtures`, leaving the initiating checkout and earlier audit worktree untouched. Executions used macOS arm64 Go 1.27.0, QUIC v1, real TLS and recovery, `testing/synctest`, fixed 5 ms simulated RTT, and a one-second Dial context. The new fixture's 4096-byte bidirectional transfer has deadlines on both streams; the original MITM test's 500 KiB payload is untouched.

These are controlled packet-selection/time scenarios, not byte-for-byte replay. Cryptographic randomness and runnable-goroutine ordering remain; packetization can vary. The selector parses QUIC packet boundaries inside a datagram and flips the last protected byte of the target Initial or Handshake packet. It leaves the rest of that datagram intact. Controls select eligible packets without changing the byte. Successful control handshakes can end the supply of eligible packets before a three-packet selection limit is reached.

## Evidence and interpretation

| Case | Predeclared disturbance | Dial result in virtual time | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [C1](raw/C1.log) | First server Initial packet damaged, then reliable delivery | Success at 205 ms | Authentication rejection followed by Initial probes, usable handshake data and completed transfer |
| [C2](raw/C2.log) | Same Initial selection, no damage | Success at 5 ms | Undamaged path/control completes normally |
| [C3](raw/C3.log) | First three server Handshake-bearing datagrams damaged, then reliable delivery | Success at 50 ms | Repeated authentication rejection followed by a usable Handshake probe and completed transfer |
| [C4](raw/C4.log) | Same Handshake selection, no damage | Success at 5 ms | Undamaged control completes with one eligible Handshake selection |
| [C5](raw/C5.log) | Continuous server Handshake damage through Dial expiry | Expected `context.DeadlineExceeded` at 1000 ms | Probes continue; no usable Handshake data arrives, no connection is returned, and teardown finishes |

The [derived summary](summary.json) binds each selection to original socket submission, routed mutation, receiver socket read and endpoint events. It also connects each recorded PTO expiry to the next ack-eliciting packet in the same packet-number space, its socket write and its routed datagram. [summarize.py](summarize.py) regenerates that evidence from the five `raw/C*.jsonl` captures without executing tests. Timestamps use a common virtual-time origin; same-time records are observations, not proof of a total causal order. All captured socket writes returned full length without error. Receiver checksum correlation supplies separate delivery evidence.

**Initial recovery, C1:** the damaged Initial is rejected at 5 ms. Client Initial PTO expires at 200 ms and server Initial PTO at 202.5 ms. In each case, an ack-eliciting packet is logged and submitted to the socket at the corresponding expiry time. The client successfully receives Handshake CRYPTO at 205 ms; the server receives Handshake ACK/CRYPTO at 207.5 ms. Both sides complete the 4096-byte exchange and close at 212.5 ms. The one damaged packet has a matching socket read and authentication-drop event. This contradicts a stopped Initial recovery chain for this case.

**Handshake recovery, C3:** the first damaged Handshake packet is rejected at 5 ms. Server PTOs expire at 17.5 and 47.5 ms. Their packet construction, socket submission and routing are captured at those times. The two packets from the first probe round are also damaged and rejected at 20 ms. After the selection limit is exhausted, usable Handshake CRYPTO reaches the client at 50 ms. The server receives ACK/CRYPTO at 52.5 ms; transfer and both closes finish at 57.5 ms. All three damaged datagrams have matching receiver reads and authentication drops. This contradicts a missing probe/submission or unusable recovery retransmission for this case.

**Deadline, C5:** server Handshake PTOs expire at 17.5, 47.5, 107.5, 227.5, 467.5 and 947.5 ms. Each has a corresponding ack-eliciting packet and successful socket write. Thirteen Handshake datagrams are damaged before Dial returns at 1000 ms; their client authentication drops are retained. Both connection-close events occur at 1000 ms. One additional Handshake datagram is selected during teardown after the deadline assertion, bringing the full capture's selection count to fourteen; it is not a fourteenth pre-deadline authentication failure. Continuous damage leaves no usable Handshake recovery opportunity, so this expected caller timeout is not a transport bug.

The diagnostic obligations come from [RFC 9002 §6.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9002.html#section-6.2): PTO backoff and ack-eliciting probes must be considered alongside amplification limits and handshake key availability. None of these cases exhibits an unexplained missing probe or unusable retransmission once reliable delivery resumes. The permanent assertions use the public completion/data/error outcomes; they do not hard-code these internal timer values or event sequences. The one-second success budget describes this fixture under the repository's current transport configuration, not a universal protocol guarantee for arbitrary recovery implementations or networks.

## Capture limits and permanent tests

[initial-capture.patch](initial-capture.patch) retains the instrumented fixture/helper for C1–C4; [deadline-capture.patch](deadline-capture.patch) retains the version including C5. Both are standalone additions against parent `16423f27`, not cumulative patches or patches to apply over the final active test. Commands, source hashes, binary hashes, toolchains, platforms and relevant environment values are retained in `raw/C*-command.json`. C1–C4 use the earlier test name `TestHandshakeRecoversFromCorruption`; the final matrix and C5 use `TestHandshakeCorruption`. Four compile-only invocations succeeded: initial capture draft, completed Initial/Handshake capture, deadline capture, and final race build. The initial draft was never executed.

Temporary instrumentation serializes qlog events while their borrowed data is valid, captures copied datagrams and socket read/write results, and writes retained records at cleanup. It adds allocation, locking and serialization. The socket wrapper hides optional buffer-setting methods and produced the disclosed warning in diagnostic logs; it does not change simulated packet bytes or configure a real kernel buffer. Final validation removes the wrapper and all temporary recorders. No temporary capture code remains active.

The simulated link intentionally isolates recovery from the live MITM proxy: mutations retain the endpoint address and fixed latency, instead of taking the original proxy-bypassing immediate replacement path. There is no real UDP scheduler, GSO, ECN or proxy source-address change in these cases. Simnet copies datagram bytes before routing, buffers delivery, and enforces a 1400-byte default link MTU; path-MTU discovery is disabled to avoid unrelated probing in this small handshake fixture. These results cannot rule out an issue in the historical proxy path or a different packetization/corruption sequence. CRC32c matching is supported by direction, length and time; it is not a collision-free packet identity.

The final tests assert that the requested damage actually occurred, bounded damage permits Dial and bidirectional transfer, and continuous damage yields the expected context error and no returned connection. Transport/listener/link shutdown remains inside the synctest bubble, which must finish with its goroutines terminated. The failure-outcome case adds coverage; it does not teach the existing randomized success test to accept timeouts. There was no failing recovery obligation to fix, so no artificial production mutation was introduced to manufacture a red/green cycle.

## Validation, budget and disposition

All five final cases passed once without temporary tracing and with race detection: [command/build receipt](raw/final-race-command.json), [output](raw/final-race.log). `go vet ./integrationtests/self` passed. Formatting and lint results are recorded in the review receipt. No full suite, randomized test, CI rerun or merge was performed.

The [ledger](LEDGER.md) accounts for the entire continuation: C1–C4 are four focused executions, C5 adds one, and final race validation adds five leaf-case executions. Including the prior two constructed cases, cumulative focused usage is **12**, four beyond the original eight under the maintainer's explicit useful-extension authorization. Original random usage remains **4/4**; no random allocation was extended. The additional cases and their purposes were declared before execution.

Retain the permanent test improvement and the evidence. No transport change is justified by these results. The historical question now specifically concerns an uncaptured corruption sequence, real proxy/UDP behavior, or an execution condition absent from this simulated network. A future historical-cause claim needs a retained failing sequence and a demonstrated gap in the recovery/delivery chain; passing these constructed cases is not a resolution of #46.
