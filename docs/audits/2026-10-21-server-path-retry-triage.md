## Verified behavior

The existing path-manager and amplification tests pass. The [archived reproduction](2026-10-21-server-path-retry-triage/reproduction_test.go.txt) uses the existing server fixture, concrete packet packer, real recovery handler and captured socket writes. It advances receive timestamps and invokes the receive/emission boundary; it does not run a deployment, the full connection event loop, or real UDP/TLS traffic. The first challenge packet is acknowledged through real recovery, while its PATH_RESPONSE is withheld. Later candidate packets at 100 ms, 1 s, 5 s and 60 s produce no retry in three cases: an initial compact challenge, an initial padded challenge, and a padded challenge sent after a compact response establishes reachability. Matching responses at 61 s provide positive controls: a compact response starts expanded validation, while a padded response triggers the existing path-switch callback and increments the path generation.

The loss characterization separately confirms that one challenge loss retires the candidate and its connection ID. Subsequent traffic creates a different candidate, and its predecessor's response is ignored. These are distinct defects in retry/lifetime behavior, not evidence of an amplification-budget failure.

## Decision record

| Question | Chosen contract | Reason and tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adopt unchanged, adapt, defer or reject? | Adapt the retry concept and tests. | The fork has confirmed stalls, but its byte accounting, emission ownership and two-stage validation need more than a direct cherry-pick. |
| What triggers retries? | Accepted candidate-path traffic after the next-probe deadline. Timers independently expire attempts; they do not send challenges. | Matches the reported failure and upstream's bounded scope. A silent candidate is abandoned; probing it without further traffic is outside this change. |
| How quickly? | A frozen conservative PTO-based interval, doubled after each committed probe. | Avoids using the old path's possibly tiny RTT as proof that the new path can respond quickly. This is deliberately more conservative than copying upstream's initial-RTT interval. |
| What stops an attempt? | An absolute deadline for each required validation stage; ordinary traffic, ACKs, losses and duplicate responses cannot renew it. | Bounds unanswered work even if the peer keeps sending. Genuine compact-response progress earns one MTU-validation stage. |
| How much state? | Keep the existing three-candidate limit and at most 16 committed challenge records per candidate. | Explicit storage bounds, independent of assumptions about how slowly exponential backoff grows. |
| What does loss mean? | ACK and loss callbacks neither validate nor delete a candidate, reset backoff, or discard its tokens. | One missing packet is not proof the route is unusable. |
| Which responses count? | Any retained emitted token for the live attempt, received on any path, using that token's actual padded status. | Reordering is tolerated without confusing reachability with minimum-MTU validation. |
| What counts as a probe? | A constructed datagram with its actual bytes reserved, immediately before recovery registration and socket handoff. | No-packet outcomes do not consume a retry or leave an acceptable unsent token. A local write error retains the existing conservative reservation. |
| Does this need a public setting or a new worker? | No. Private policy and the existing connection goroutine/timer suffice. | Avoids expanding public API or introducing concurrent ownership for this repair. |

The protocol basis is [RFC 9000 §§8.2 and 9.4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9000.html#section-8.2): distinguish matching responses from ACKs, preserve compact-versus-expanded validation, allow responses on other paths, and allow enough time for losses before abandonment. The exact five-second floor and 16-token cap are engineering choices for this fork, not RFC-prescribed values or performance-qualified tuning.

## Complete behavior contract

### Timing and stages

At candidate admission, snapshot `B`, the larger of the active connection's unbacked-off PTO including maximum ACK delay and the fork's initial-path PTO. Use the existing RTT/PTO definitions; do not allocate a new estimator for every packet. At the evidence revision, the initial-path PTO is 200 ms. Snapshot `D = max(5 seconds, 3 * B)`. Calculate durations with overflow-safe arithmetic. Neither value shrinks or grows in response to subsequent traffic on this attempt.

The first stage expires at admission time plus `D`. The first challenge is eligible immediately. After a probe is committed, the next retry becomes eligible after `B`, then `2B`, then `4B`, and so on, measured from the actual previous probe commitment. A late triggering packet produces at most one probe and starts its next interval from that actual send; there is no catch-up burst. A packet exactly at the next-probe time may trigger a retry if the stage has not expired. The expiry boundary is exclusive: at or beyond the stage deadline, that attempt cannot send or validate. Clamp interval arithmetic to the remaining lifetime instead of permitting overflowing shifts.

An accepted compact response makes reachability true and starts the optional MTU stage once, with a new deadline at that response's acceptance time plus `D`. If no padded challenge was previously emitted, an expanded challenge becomes eligible on the next candidate packet, including that same packet if the response arrived on the candidate path; its subsequent waits start at `B`. If padded challenges are already outstanding, keep their existing backoff schedule rather than immediately duplicating them. Retain all prior emitted-token metadata until completion or abandonment. Repeated compact responses cannot restart the stage or its schedule. A retained padded response can complete both requirements directly, including when a later compact response has already established reachability.

At `B = 200 ms`, an immediately funded stage can emit at 0, 0.2, 0.6, 1.4 and 3.0 seconds before its five-second deadline. A second stage can add at most five probes under the same parameters; the 16-record bound therefore leaves headroom. Slower bases or delayed triggering packets reduce that number. When the storage cap is reached, stop issuing challenges but keep accepting retained responses until the deadline; never evict a useful token to make room or restart its lifetime. The at-most-two-stage lifetime is bounded by `2D`; a fully successful candidate no longer needs validation tokens.

### Emission and response ownership

Keep the connection goroutine as protocol-state owner and `packetEmission` as the owner of construction, actual-byte reservation, recovery registration and socket handoff. A generated challenge is provisional until a concrete datagram is ready and its path budget has been reserved. Before that boundary, unavailable recovery capacity, an unavailable connection ID, or insufficient room to pack the frames leaves retry count, next-probe deadline and the acceptable-token set unchanged. Candidate lifetime still advances. Preserve existing error propagation for construction errors.

Record the token's padded property from the constructed UDP datagram length, not from anticipated allowance or a candidate-wide last-probe flag. Successful reservation precedes recovery registration and write. Once that boundary is crossed, retain accounting, token metadata and backoff even if the best-effort local write fails. Do not roll back destructive packing or recovery state. Release every outgoing buffer through the existing emission lifetime.

For an unvalidated address, all probe and response datagrams continue to spend only that candidate's received-byte allowance. Retries never borrow active-path credit. Duplicate or rejected packets add no credit. Reachability success can lift that allowance as today, but a compact response cannot authorize migration before minimum-MTU validation. Both validation facts are monotonic within an attempt; a late compact response cannot undo padded success.

Respond to the peer's PATH_CHALLENGE on its received path, subject to the existing send constraints. Waiting for our retry deadline must not delay that response. If there is room for the response alone but not a combined response and our due challenge, prioritize the response and leave our challenge eligible. A peer's response obligation, our retry schedule, and packet acknowledgments remain distinct.

### Expiry, capacity and migration

Add the earliest incomplete-attempt expiry to the existing connection timer, including when sending is hard-blocked or congestion-limited. Expiry must release candidate/token state and retire its probing connection ID through the existing owner even when no new candidate packet arrives. Process expiry on ordinary event-loop progress too, so continuous traffic cannot prevent it. No per-path goroutine or independent timer object is needed. Check attempt validity before accepting PATH_RESPONSE, including responses arriving on the active path; timer dispatch order must not allow an already-expired token to succeed. An already-retired attempt can never be resurrected by queued frames.

An accepted packet after abandonment may start a new attempt under normal admission rules, with a fresh attempt identity and newly accounted bytes. This is a new opportunity, not a continuation of expired token history. No guarantee of fairness among an unlimited set of competing source addresses is added. The existing three-candidate cap still applies, and matching an existing candidate takes precedence over rejecting an unseen fourth candidate. Retrying and peer-challenge responses on existing candidates remain possible at capacity.

Keep the existing five-second idle eviction behavior for otherwise eligible candidate records; do not let it prematurely replace a live incomplete attempt before its new stage deadline on a slow path. Once validation has fully succeeded, clear tokens and stop retry/attempt-expiry scheduling while retaining the established candidate behavior until migration or ordinary idle eviction. Switching paths discards other attempts and their tokens with the existing connection-ID ownership rules. Connection termination releases all attempt state without scheduling more writes or redundant connection-ID retirement after the owner is closed. An abandoned candidate does not itself close the working active connection.

Preserve the existing highest-numbered non-probing-packet migration gate, path generation, congestion/recovery reset, packet-size reset, DATAGRAM limits and MTU-discovery restart behavior. This issue does not redesign migration policy.

## Finite implementation and validation scope

Deliver one cohesive production PR: server retry state, the emission commitment boundary, response matching, timer expiry and their terminal cleanup belong together. Do not ship loss-retention before bounded expiry is present. Three ordered work slices fit inside that PR: (1) express the three failing retry scenarios and loss/late-response cases using the existing test seams; (2) implement the complete coordinated behavior and deterministic boundary tests; (3) prove the composition through the real connection timer and one controlled NAT-rebinding integration case. These are internal work slices, not newly dispatched child issues or separate architecture-handoff work.

| Acceptance family | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Retry behavior | The three reproduced stalls recover with a response to a retry; before-deadline, exact-deadline and late-arrival cases; fresh tokens; no catch-up burst; no unsolicited timer-driven retry. |
| Metadata and reordering | Earlier and latest tokens accepted while live, including on the active path; compact/padded ordering in both directions; compact duplicates do not renew deadlines or downgrade success; expired, unsent and previous-attempt tokens ignored. |
| Emission and credit | Real packer/recovery tests for per-candidate credit, replay rejection, compact/expanded datagram sizes, capacity/connection-ID/no-packet deferral, response priority, local-write reservation retention and buffer cleanup. |
| Lifetime and capacity | Fixed record bounds; candidate limit with existing-candidate activity; cap exhaustion waits for expiry; timer-driven cleanup under silence and send blocking; traffic cannot extend lifetime; loss does not retire; renewed admission uses a fresh identity; switch/close cleanup. |
| Shipped composition | A real-loop virtual-time expiry test and a controlled UDP/TLS NAT-rebinding test that withholds the first response, requires a later challenge, and verifies migration/continued delivery. Retain existing migration, amplification and MTU regressions. |

Run focused unit/connection tests once, the focused race tests, the controlled integration case and repository-required checks for the resulting change. Test the declared behavior, not a copy of production scheduling logic. No sustained benchmark campaign, new platform matrix, generic path framework, new public configuration, client-probe redesign, global upstream sync or unrelated path-limit port is included. The current evidence is sufficient to specify the repair; it does not claim the proposed implementation or its UDP/TLS behavior has already passed.
