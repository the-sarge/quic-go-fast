# Native endpoint efficiency and admission implementation plan

**Date:** 2026-11-05. **Status:** owner-authorized scoped recovery-accounting amendment; independent slice audits passed; 4/5 slices complete (N0, N1, N2 and N4 #1213); N3 #1202 is the frontier. **Identity:** `QGF-MP646-20261024-N`. **Parent:** [ordinary endpoint regression (#1193)](https://github.com/the-sarge/quic-go-fast/issues/1193). **Program:** [multipath #646](2026-10-24-multipath-program.md). **Normative scope:** current outcome, boundaries, invariants, acceptance evidence, blockers and stops. **Audit history:** [original handoff](../audits/2026-11-04-native-endpoint-efficiency-handoff.md) and [recovery-accounting re-audit](../audits/2026-11-05-native-recovery-accounting-handoff.md).

## Goal and accepted boundary

Restore ordinary-peer costs on granted native endpoints within the existing Reno-plan tolerances and implement the owner-approved public/stateless admission contract for issue #1141. The native endpoint module retains all admission, local ownership, deadline, revocation, completion and grant-return policy behind its existing private interface. Remove work proportional to unused participant capacity, per-operation command/ack rendezvous, and repeated scans of unresolved completion records during capacity queries. N4 changes only the representation of recovery tracking capacity; recovery policy remains in the connection goroutine. The connection goroutine continues to own QUIC policy and recovery; one native writer continues to own socket writes and write deadlines. Public activation remains R19 #1039.

The [endpoint ownership contract and parameters](2026-10-24-multipath-endpoint-plan.md#endpoint-ownership-contract), [Reno product tolerances](2026-10-24-multipath-reno-plan.md#accepted-product-thresholds), ADRs [0001](0001-upstream-compatibility.md), [0004](0004-packet-emission-ownership.md) and [0011](0011-recovery-registration-prefix.md) remain binding. This plan selects the synchronized-module equivalent permitted by the endpoint contract: “It can be an event loop plus native worker or an equivalent mechanism.” One control owner does not require that every short state transaction execute on a dedicated goroutine. Control still progresses independently of a potentially blocked writer.

Non-goals: change the 4,096 participant, 128 request, 8 MiB payload, 256 completion-record, per-class or four-per-connection limits; change writer-service fairness or persistent data/control fairness, the 10 ms grant lease, 1 ms retry floor, B or its 20 ms tolerance; add queues, protocol workers, busy polling, multiple native writers, native I/O on a connection goroutine, public interfaces, a new platform or a special ordinary performance route; change recovery formulas, wire behavior, GSO semantics or the approved admission outcome for issue #1141; relax performance thresholds. The owner-waived operational ceilings are governed by the scoped waiver below, not by a product or safety relaxation. Unregistered endpoints retain their existing path.

## Decisions

### N1: a sparse participant index, owned with canonical identity

Keep slot identity and validity authoritative in the endpoint state. Add a bounded, control-owned index of occupied slots or equivalent intrusive links, maintained at attachment and final retirement rather than rebuilt per operation. It includes both fixed response participants and retiring participants until their canonical slot is actually released. The hot arbitration and retry-deadline walks visit occupied participants, preserving the existing circular slot order relative to each admission cursor, path turns and control allowances. Cold attachment/free-slot discovery may retain its bounded table scan. Service order and capacity accumulation for an already selected large request do not change.

The index contains references to canonical participants, not copied eligibility, result, grant or lifecycle state. Its storage is bounded by the existing participant allowance; there is no per-request or per-retry allocation and no new payload allowance. Installation, retirement, slot reuse, withdrawal, sealing and fault cleanup remain one endpoint-owned lifecycle. This removes the diagnostic probe's per-arbitration rebuilding allocation and its remaining full-capacity scan.

### N2: direct one-shot admission with persistent-record protection

Apply the [owner-approved issue #1141 contract](../agents/native-endpoint-admission-1141.md) within the same endpoint admission owner. The class request maxima (104/8/8/8) and byte maxima (6 MiB/1 MiB/512 KiB/512 KiB) already partition the global allowance. Do not add a second count/byte reservation pool. An authorized, valid public/stateless request whose retry floor has elapsed admits when those existing class, participant and global allowances permit it and a completion record remains beyond the selected persistent grant's protection. Genuine exhaustion still refuses immediately with no caller-buffer transfer, pending waiter or hidden retry.

Before consuming a shared completion record, reclaim invalid/expired/backed-off grants and consider already registered eligible persistent producers through the existing arbitration owner. Protect one free record for a selected persistent grant, including a legal request accumulating class capacity: direct admission needs two free records with that grant and one without it. Count reserved-but-uncommitted admissions and terminal records held by stalled consumers as used. A materialized persistent reservation has already consumed its one record and cannot be charged twice. Derive this protection from the authoritative grant and records rather than adding another reservation owner or cached free-record counter.

Public/stateless calls do not register persistent readiness, consume its selected grant, drain its wake notification, renew its lease or alter its retry floor. Their fixed participant records retain the existing local backpressure cooldown. Persistent data/control admission keeps its round-robin/path policy and large-request progress. Writer service remains unchanged across all classes, including at most two non-data request turns before available data. Both admission paths allocate/commit through the same record, count/byte, queue, deadline and result-lifetime code; this is a class policy inside one owner, not another endpoint or production performance bypass.

Preserve response authorization and epochs for reset, Retry, version negotiation, refusal/INVALID_TOKEN and cached-close responses; live connection close remains connection-control. Preserve public zero-length datagrams, invalid/oversize validation, actual-native-return borrow and error identities. All transitions remain under the existing command-owned endpoint in N2 and migrate together in N3. No runtime code from the issue #1141 diagnostic is an accepted baseline.

### N3: synchronized endpoint control with independent progress

Make endpoint state a single synchronized module. Short private interface operations enter its one mutex, perform the existing validated state transaction and required progress, then leave. Remove the command channel and per-operation ack rendezvous together; do not keep a second command-mediated mutation path. The mutex is the serialization mechanism, not a second state owner. A private closure-based helper may remain inside the module if it cannot escape, reenter control, perform native I/O or invoke an external callback.

Factor the current progress pass into one bounded state-owner operation shared by inline transactions and one independent progress goroutine. It reconciles an observed native result, processes sealing/revocation and expired queued work, reclaims/arbitrates grants, services deposited closes and claims the next request in the accepted order. Required timer work runs even without producers; sustained producer traffic also encounters the same overdue/expiry owner rather than postponing it. Keep the current observation semantics: an absent-fact overdue decision is irreversible, a timely fact visible at inspection is accepted by its writer timestamp, and late publication never erases an already recorded fault.

Only the progress goroutine manages its timer's Reset/Stop and wait loop. Mutations signal a bounded coalesced wakeup; notifications carry no payload or policy. Compute the next deadline from locked state, then recheck through that owner after a wake. A mutation between computing/resetting the timer and entering the wait must leave either a pending wake or an already observed state change. Stale/coalesced wakeups are harmless; no notification may be required once per event. Preserve current request, grant lease, retry floor, queued expiry and deposited-close deadline sources.

The native writer never takes the control mutex and never calls policy. Claim still installs the current token before handoff, including deadline setup in that token's lifetime. Dispatch under control must be nonblocking by the single-current-token invariant and bounded work-slot capacity; do not turn a full channel into a lock-held wait. Native SetWriteDeadline, native sends/fallbacks, native return, sink execution, waiting for public completion, waiting for deposited closes, writer join and the grant-return callback are outside the mutex. Buffer release, credit completion and bounded result publication remain endpoint-owned; audit their concrete implementations for lock ordering rather than assuming that all callbacks are safe.

A terminal state under the mutex excludes new transactions/publication before the progress goroutine closes work or diagnostic channels. Exactly one progress owner closes them, joins the writer, calls the internal grant-return callback outside control/global lock inversion, and finally publishes `done`. Waiting for a close or join must first copy immutable wait handles under control, then release it. The grant registry lock used during configuration must never be acquired while holding endpoint control in the reverse order. A captured producer after sealing cannot restart admission, dispatch, timer/deadline work or publication. The public write borrow remains retained until the native operation actually returns, including overdue quarantine.

**Owner's design:** adopted. Restructure the existing state owner to use a short synchronized transaction rather than surrounding it with completion queues, optimistic copies or cached health flags. It removes cross-context request/ack obligations while retaining the writer's necessary fact handoff and the independent progress wake. No closed public or ownership contract changes in N3; N2 applies only the explicitly approved admission amendment. N1 first removes avoidable capacity-sized work from the proposed critical section; running the old scans under a mutex would merely transfer their cost to callers.

### N0: publish and repair the existing measurement deliverable

The R18 #1038 branch is source material, not an accepted dependency. Extract only the ordinary-cost fixture, Python driver and its tests; do not import its journal or historical claims. Review it against the accepted six cells, matched workloads, exact toolchain, candidate identities, tolerances and program accounting before publishing it as a reusable verification aid. The source/history disposition is in the audit.

**Accepted metric decision:** define the ordinary comparison's p99 send cost as elapsed application `Stream.Write` / `SendDatagram` call time. Call it **application send-call latency** in the driver, summaries and qualification receipt; do not describe it as native-send completion or peer delivery. `SendDatagram` can return after queue admission, and `Stream.Write` return likewise does not promise native return or acknowledgment. Retain the numeric ceiling max(110% of baseline, baseline + 1 ms) and the separate application control-echo latency metric.

This is an explicit clarification of an ambiguous accepted metric, not an assertion that the unmerged fixture already defined it. Application call time measures the caller cost this ordinary-peer comparison is intended to protect. Receiver-counted useful goodput, CPU/useful byte, timeout/drop/memory and fair-progress evidence still constrain the behavior after the call; a quick return cannot compensate for lost useful delivery. Native ownership, actual-return borrow release, absolute deadlines, independent expiry, overdue faults and join remain separately required by the endpoint contract and N3's existing E6 qualification. Those cases establish their native contract, not a comparative p99 native-send statistic. A new comparative native-latency metric would need a justified start/finish boundary and instrumentation on both routes; that extra maintained measurement obligation is not warranted by the present caller-cost decision.

Preserve the existing raw offered-traffic, reset-return and background-useful-byte fields in N0 and expose them in the R18 summary to assess the independent admission outcome. Useful goodput remains receiver-counted useful content; controls are application echo latency. No transport acknowledgment, API return or local completion is substituted for useful delivery. Rename the misleading `send_completion` field consistently in this not-yet-published fixture/driver/tests and emit explicit metric-boundary metadata. Frozen historical diagnostics retain their original field names with the audit explanation; they are not qualification results under the clarified contract.

Repair observed driver failure boundaries before reuse: charge an attempted observation before launching roles, retain its failure and elapsed occupied time when a role/check fails, enforce the supplied remaining instance/host-time allowance before another invocation, and return nonzero when a required case is absent, invalid, contaminated without an admitted replacement, or below tolerance. Preserve the fixed 36-observation campaign and its established contamination-only replacement rule; a replacement must fit the driver’s supplied finite reservation and have a recorded cause; the scoped waiver below governs how that reservation is supplied for N3 #1202 and R18 #1038. Smoke/diagnostic pairs must be labeled, charged and summarized separately and cannot satisfy the counted inventory. Existing test-only activation remains confined to the fixture; no production test hook or public activation is added.

### N4: history-owned membership for unresolved completion identities

Keep `localCompletions.records` as the sole token-identity/lifetime map. Let each `sentPacketHistory` own a lazily allocated boolean membership slice aligned with its retained packet-number slots and a count of true members. A member means exactly that one unresolved local token is represented by that current history slot; it remains true when the packet pointer becomes an interior hole, and is unrelated to packet-distance qualification. Ordinary histories without local registrations never allocate this slice. The connection goroutine remains the only mutation context.

Compute each handler's tracked count as `sum(current history lengths) + len(local.records) - sum(current history unresolved-member counts)`, with the existing bounded root/path aggregation. The sum visits at most the three current spaces per retained path, never every token. `CanRegisterLocalPackets`, registration, send mode/allowance, PTO skip admission and path-probe admission retain their existing central capacity query and refusal rules. There is no caller-side cache, preauthorized batch credit or second capacity limit.

History insertion appends false membership for skipped numbers and path-probe markers, and true membership for a pending local registration; first allocation includes false entries for any existing slots. Packet retirement clears the packet pointer without clearing membership. Prefix cleanup removes membership and adjusts the count while it already removes those slots; release of a whole retained span clears both. Completion validates owner, token, epoch, outcome and time first, then clears membership only in the matching current packet-number-space epoch and PN window, consuming the map entry exactly once before any early return or contradiction result. Invalid or duplicate completion changes neither. Dropped/replaced histories contribute no membership to the aggregate, so their unresolved tokens remain charged through the existing map without a scan or lifecycle callback. A late old-epoch completion cannot clear a replacement slot at the same PN. 0-RTT and 1-RTT continue to share one application history/epoch.

All slot append, trim and reset operations own membership updates inside history methods. Keep the boolean slice absent on the ordinary non-local route; on the local route its logical length tracks the retained span, its reusable capacity is bounded by history capacity, and no registration/frame/native-buffer pointer is retained by it. Count reads are allocation-free and constant in token population; cleanup work is proportional to removed slots, which are already traversed. Report added constant struct size and peak allocated membership capacity, including slice capacity rather than only length. No history retention, tracking ceiling, record limit or pool lifetime changes.

**Owner's design:** change the recovery owner's history representation directly. This is not a side channel beside the owner. The sole new consistency obligation is membership/count alignment with history slots; insertion, validated completion and prefix/reset removal are its three central mutation seams. It removes repeated identity-map traversal without introducing a worker, lock, timer or cross-context signal. Reusing `unqualifiedLocal` is rejected because ACK qualification and path probes have different lifetimes from unresolved completion identity. A flag on `packet` alone is insufficient because pooling and interior holes can outlive the packet object. Both alternatives are rejected on merit.

## Representation and preservation contract

Runtime domain: the already accepted private granted endpoint on exact native UDP resources; ordinary QUIC v1/v2, private negotiated draft-21 v1, their data/control/probe/close producers, stateless responses and public writes. Existing QUIC parsers, authentication, public configuration validators and private request constructors own external representations. Endpoint control validates internal participant, admission serial, epoch and current-token identity. Universal runtime ownership/order guarantees apply within this domain; the finite tests below are regression evidence, not exhaustive proof. Linux amd64 is the only qualified venue; hosted compilation/unit coverage does not add platform support.

The fixture accepts its declared JSON records and fixed cell/profile vocabulary through Python/Go JSON parsers and validates the required semantics. Its performance evidence is example-level on the selected host/toolchain/workloads. Neither a smoke nor a benchmark establishes a universal throughput claim. There is no persisted library state or restart format; fresh endpoint construction, slot reuse and resource re-grant are the corresponding reconstruction boundaries.

All slices preserve packet construction before recovery registration and registration before handoff, immutable local-completion identity, known/unknown/unsubmitted distinctions, native borrow fencing, fault quarantine, bounded nonblocking diagnostics, readiness coalescing, grant leases and independent expiry. No recovery, emission or response adapter becomes another endpoint policy owner.
